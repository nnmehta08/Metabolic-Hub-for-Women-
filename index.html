What this is

Menopause is associated with cardiometabolic shifts (blood pressure, lipids, glucose regulation, and body fat distribution). This page organizes the core metrics clinicians use for risk assessment and makes them easier to review together.

Missed opportunities this hub targets

  • Central adiposity not measured (waist circumference) when BMI is “not too high.”
  • “Normal HDL” interpreted as protective despite menopause‑related HDL function changes.
  • BP, triglycerides, and A1C reviewed separately (missing the metabolic syndrome cluster).
  • Female‑specific risk enhancers not incorporated into global risk discussion.
  • Kidney markers (eGFR, urine ACR) omitted despite CKM risk overlap.

If you build this into a product later, the goal is simple: organize key measures into one view and highlight when the pattern changes.

Cardiometabolic screening checklist (postmenopause + obesity focus)

Use this as a structured review. Add dates and results over time.

Domain What to assess Why it matters / notes
Adiposity
BMI (at least annually)
Waist circumference (especially if BMI < 35)
Threshold often used: ≥88 cm (≥35 in) in women
 Central fat redistribution is common in the menopausal transition and can add ASCVD risk beyond BMI alone.
Blood pressure At least annually; document home readings if available.
Elevated: ≥140/90 warrants evaluation.
 Obesity correlates with rising systolic BP; later life control rates can be worse in women.
Lipids Fasting or nonfasting lipid panel (per guideline cadence).
Track: TC, LDL‑C, TG, HDL‑C; consider ApoB if available.
 LDL‑C and ApoB can rise across the final menstrual period; atherogenic dyslipidemia often coexists with metabolic syndrome.
Glucose / diabetes A1C and/or fasting glucose; consider OGTT when indicated.
Overweight/obesity often triggers risk‑based screening intervals.
 Diabetes confers disproportionately higher CV risk in women than men.
Metabolic syndrome Check for clustering: central obesity + BP + TG + HDL + glucose.
≥3 of 5 criteria = MetS.
 MetS prevalence increases with menopause and is independently associated with incident CVD in postmenopausal women.
Global risk Calculate 10‑year ASCVD risk (PCE / PREVENT).
Document baseline and changes over time.
 Standard calculators may underestimate risk in women; context matters.
Risk enhancers Female‑specific and general enhancers:
  • Early menopause / POI
  • Adverse pregnancy outcomes (preeclampsia, GDM)
  • PCOS
  • Autoimmune/inflammatory disease
  • Depression
 These can shift borderline/intermediate risk toward earlier pharmacotherapy or more intensive prevention.
Selected markers Consider when appropriate:
  • Coronary artery calcium (CAC)
  • Lp(a)
  • hs‑CRP
 Can refine risk when decisions are uncertain (e.g., intermediate risk).
Kidney function (CKM) eGFR and urine albumin‑creatinine ratio. Kidney markers add prognostic information alongside metabolic and CV screening.

Tip: capture trend lines (e.g., waist, BP, TG, A1C) rather than single snapshots—patterns often emerge over 6–24 months.

Pattern signals that are easy to miss

These are not diagnoses—just “review together” prompts.

1) Central adiposity + borderline labs

  • Waist circumference rising while BMI changes little
  • Triglycerides creeping up (≥150 mg/dL)
  • A1C drifting upward (even within “prediabetes” range)

2) Metabolic syndrome clustering

  • BP elevated + TG elevated + low HDL + hyperglycemia
  • Document criteria count (0–5) at each visit

3) “Reassuring HDL” in the wrong context

  • HDL‑C may look normal, but overall atherogenic profile worsens across menopause
  • Consider ApoB/non‑HDL‑C discussion with clinician when available

4) Risk enhancers not captured in calculators

  • Early menopause/POI, pregnancy complications, PCOS
  • Inflammatory disease, depression

Questions to ask
Focused on missed opportunities + patterns
  • Can we measure/track my waist circumference along with BMI?
  • Do my BP/lipids/A1C meet criteria for metabolic syndrome?
  • What is my 10‑year ASCVD risk, and does it underestimate risk for me?
  • Do I have any female‑specific risk enhancers (early menopause, pregnancy history, PCOS)?
  • Should we check kidney markers (eGFR, urine ACR)?
  • Would CAC, Lp(a), or hs‑CRP help refine my risk?
